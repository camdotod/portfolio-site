What place does artificial intelligence (AI) have in my design process?

AI has become the subject of much discussion in the last few years, particularly for designers, as we grapple with how new tools and programs will affect how we work. This is a question I’ve asked myself, and I feel it is important for me to address how I approach it in my work.

For me, AI does not have a place in my design process or in any of my creative pursuits.

There are a number of ethical and moral reasons why I choose to avoid AI, but from a purely functional standpoint, it’s because I believe that truly considering every decision that goes into making something is crucial to the integrity of my design process.

AI tools are often intended to reduce time spent on “repetitive” or “mundane” tasks, but in my experience, I often feel that these tasks are actually a valuable part of the process. Whether it’s making a prototype, editing a photo, or writing an email, there is always something to learn or practice. Seeing the result of the choices I’ve made prompts me to ask myself “why did(n’t) that work?” or “is this what I want to create?”

So by handing these tasks off, I feel I would lose that chance to evaluate and reflect. I see the design process as a framework for learning, and as someone who still has much to learn, I believe it would be a disservice to the development of my craft to use artificial intelligence.
